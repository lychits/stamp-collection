# Used common bird stamps: current resale guidance (research snapshot 2026-10-28)

## Bottom line

For ordinary used/postally cancelled 1970s–1990s bird stamps from Cuba, USSR, Vietnam, Nicaragua, Poland, Hungary, Namibia and Australia, a defensible **working resale range is about US$0.01–0.08 per stamp when sold as a sorted/group lot**. The practical center for ordinary common examples is **US$0.02–0.05 each**. For an isolated online single, a seller may need to ask more to cover handling, but that is not evidence of a sale and should not be treated as the stamp’s resale value. **Soiled, torn, heavily cancelled, thinned, stuck-down, or otherwise faulty examples: US$0–0.02 each, often effectively no independent value; include as a free extra or bulk-lot filler.**

These are cautious broad guidance bands, not country-specific completed-sale quotations. The evidence found in this four-search pass is thin for the eight countries and is mainly fixed-price dealer inventory plus auction-catalogue estimates—not completed hammer results. Do not use mint catalogue values or catalogue-condition values to raise these estimates.

**Condition adjustment:** For clean, genuinely postally used stamps with legible, unobtrusive cancels and sound perforations, use the middle/top of the band; for indistinct or heavy cancels, staining, toning, creases, short/missing perfs, hinge remnants, or grime, use the bottom or zero. CTO should be identified separately from postal use; CTO lots can realize less. A complete, attractive set may sell as a unit above the per-stamp bulk rate, but no premium should be assumed for ordinary sets without sold comparables.

## Price evidence (currency: US dollars unless noted)

1. **Eagle Stamps, worldwide fixed-price stock list** — live page states last updated **2026-09-19** (accessed 2026-10-28). Exact indications: Hungary #1233–35, 3 used bird stamps, **$0.15 total** (5 cents each); Poland #2075a–7a, two Kestrel/Falcon attached pairs, **$0.20** (about 5 cents each); Poland #2075a–7a, #2078, a five-stamp partial set, **$0.25** (5 cents each); mixed topical lot #549, 25 used/CTO birds, **$0.30** (1.2 cents each). Those prices are offers in a dealer stock list, not confirmed completed sales. They are nevertheless useful low-level grouped-price anchors and substantially more relevant than mint catalogue values. The page’s examples for Australia (unrelated, not birds) include five used stamps with two moderately damaged at $0.25 (~5 cents each) and six used stamps at $0.65 (~10.8 cents each); context only.
   - Source and fetched extract: [Eagle Stamps — Worldwide](https://www.eaglestamps.com/flist.htm) ([saved extract](sources/pricing-01-eagle-worldwide-birds.md)).
2. **Mowbray Collectables Postal Auction 541**, closing **7 December 2021** — catalogue records a Nicaragua 1991 Birds set (lot 323) and other thematic material. This is a pre-sale catalogue, not a realization list; no hammer price was returned in the fetched content. It verifies that bird sets appear in specialist auction offerings but cannot establish a resale amount.
   - [Auction catalogue PDF](https://www.mowbrays.co.nz/files/catalogues/cat_541.pdf) ([saved extract](sources/pricing-02-mowbray-auction.md)).
3. **SellMyStamps realised-results archive** — describes final hammer prices from past T&T Auctions and explicitly distinguishes realized results from estimates, while noting condition/provenance effects. The page is useful as the right type of evidence source, but the fetched landing page did not expose a matching comparable for these common bird issues.
   - [Stamp Auction Results & Realised Prices Archive](https://sellmystamps.co.uk/auction-results) ([saved extract](sources/pricing-03-sellmystamps-results.md)).

## Identification / catalog context (not price evidence)

[BirdTheme’s country pages](http://www.birdtheme.org/) are useful to confirm issuing country, issue date, bird subject, and identify candidate sets. [StampWorld](https://www.stampworld.com/) may show catalog/marketplace fields for used stamps, but its displayed prices are not completed-sale evidence. Neither a catalogue value nor a current dealer ask should be described as a market realization.

## Limitations and recommended comp practice

- Four searches returned very few genuinely comparable completed results for ordinary used bird stamps in the eight target countries. No country-specific completed-sale data was verified in the fetched sources. The ranges above are therefore cautious estimation bands anchored by grouped dealer prices; they are not a claim that each issue has sold at those amounts.
- The Eagle prices are fixed-price stock offers and may be unsold; regard them as an upper-ish retail anchor, not a hammer result. The Mowbray source is an auction catalogue and gives estimates/offer information rather than final prices. The SellMyStamps source describes hammer-price evidence but yielded no matching lot from its landing page.
- For a defensible issue-level valuation, match the exact Scott/Michel/SG number, genuine postal cancellation vs CTO, condition, and set completeness, then record several recent completed sales (sale date, currency, lot size, realized/hammer price, buyer premium/shipping where known). Convert a grouped realization to per-stamp value only as an average and discount for mixed quality/duplication.
- Resale to an individual may differ from dealer buy-in value or net proceeds after marketplace fees, postage, and time. At 1–5 cents per stamp, selling and shipping individually is usually uneconomic.
